Welcome to Marriane's Autobiography

I am Marriane Joy Bartolata, a fourth-year Bachelor of Science in Nursing. I am someone who believes that life is not simply about achieving personal success, but about becoming a person who can be proud of the life she has built and the people she has been able to help along the way.

I have always been someone who takes my goals seriously. When I set my mind on something, I try my best to remain committed to it, even when the process becomes difficult. I find fulfillment in knowing that I am working toward something meaningful. For me, having goals gives direction to my life, while discipline and consistency help me turn those goals into something real.

Over the years, I have learned that the person I want to become is shaped not only by my achievements, but also by the principles I choose to live by. I value responsibility, commitment, humility, perseverance, and service. I believe that doing something well means giving it my genuine effort, even when nobody is watching. I also believe that mistakes are not something to be ashamed of. They are opportunities to learn, become wiser, and do better the next time.

One of the things that motivates me most is my family. A large part of what I strive for is not only for myself, but for the people I love. I dream of reaching a point in life where I can give my family a comfortable life—not necessarily one defined by extravagance, but one where they can rest without constantly worrying about what comes next. I want to be able to give back to the people who have sacrificed, supported, and believed in me.

This desire has taught me to appreciate hard work. I know that the life I envision will not simply happen. It will require patience, discipline, courage, and many sacrifices along the way. There may be seasons when I have to choose between comfort now and something better in the future. I am willing to work through those seasons because I believe that some things are worth striving for.

At the same time, I do not want to spend my entire life chasing success and forget to actually live. I value the simple moments that make life meaningful. I enjoy spending time with my friends, playing pickleball, talking about random things, laughing over small moments, and celebrating both ordinary and special days together. These moments remind me that while goals give me something to work toward, relationships give me reasons to appreciate the journey.

I also value service and leadership. To me, being a leader does not always mean being the loudest person or having the highest position. Sometimes, leadership is simply choosing to be dependable, helping when you can, taking responsibility, and making yourself useful to others. I want to be someone whom people can trust—not because I am perfect, but because I try to do what is right and remain committed to the people and responsibilities entrusted to me.

My faith is also an important part of how I see life. I believe that the abilities, opportunities, and people God has placed in my life are not without purpose. There are things I can control through my own effort, and there are things I have to surrender and trust to God. I want to pursue my dreams with determination while remembering that my plans are ultimately part of something greater than myself.

I am still learning what kind of life I want to build. I know that I want a life that is meaningful, stable, and comfortable—not only for myself, but for the people I love. I want to be successful without losing my values, ambitious without becoming selfish, and strong without becoming insensitive to others.

For me, success is not simply having a good career, earning well, or reaching a certain position. It is being able to look at the life I have built and know that I remained faithful to the principles that mattered to me. It is being able to provide for my family, enjoy the company of people I love, serve others, make memories, and have peace knowing that I gave my best to the life I was given.

I am still becoming the person I hope to be. I have many things to learn, many goals to pursue, and many challenges ahead. But I want to continue moving forward with purpose—with discipline when things are difficult, gratitude when things are good, courage when I am afraid, and faith when I cannot see what comes next.

Ultimately, I do not just want to build a successful life.

I want to build a life worth coming home to—and a life that allows the people I love to feel that all the hard work was worth it.
